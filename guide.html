Here’s how to settle into DumasWriter on your Mac.

A guide to DumasWriter 0.8.4 for Mac.

YOUR FIRST FEW MINUTES

01   Install the app 02   Give your writing a home 03   Begin a book 04   Find your atmosphere 05   Make it comfortable 06   Share your words 07   Keep your work close
01

A place on your Mac.

Install DumasWriter from the Mac App Store, then open it from your Applications folder. Check the store listing for current system requirements, price, and availability.

Choose Show me around for a short introduction, or begin exploring at your own pace. You can return to the tour through Help → Tutorial whenever you like.

Your writing happens in the installed Mac app. This website is here to introduce it and help you get started.

02

Give your words a home.

Choose a Library folder for your writing. DumasWriter keeps your books and chapters there as ordinary files on your Mac: a book is a folder, and a chapter is a Markdown file inside it.

Press ⌘O to open the Library. Your books and loose papers appear on the left, the selected book’s chapters in the middle, and notes on the right. Double-click a chapter to open it.

Pick a folder you’ll recognize later. Your files remain accessible in Finder. If you choose a folder managed by a cloud service, that service controls its syncing.

03

One book.
One first line.

In the Library, choose New Book… and give your story a name. Select your book and add a chapter. ⌘N starts a new chapter or paper, depending on where you’re working.

Open the chapter and begin. Your changes are saved automatically as plain Markdown a couple of seconds after you stop typing. You can return to the Library whenever you want to move between chapters.

Keep ideas beside the draft with ⇧⌘M. Chapters, papers, and whole books can each have notes of their own, saved separately from the manuscript.

Already started elsewhere? Choose File → Import to bring in a DOCX, RTF, or text document. Review the text before saving an imported copy. Formatting is simplified; images, comments, and tracked changes are not preserved as editing features. Your original file stays untouched.

04

Find a place
you want to stay.

Open Scene → Browse Scenes… and choose a background. A forest, a misty valley, a night beneath the stars — find the one that suits the page in front of you.

In Settings → Scene, choose how much the room responds: Still, Gentle, or Alive. Adjust the atmosphere to match your mood and the way you like to work.

A scene is a place to write, not a choice you have to commit to. Change it whenever the next chapter calls for something different.

05

Make the page feel like you.

The corner sound button opens controls for music and nature sounds. Adjust them separately, choose a music style, or turn them down for quiet.

Open Settings → Text to choose your font, size, spacing, and column width. Try typewriter scrolling to keep your current line comfortable, or paragraph focus to soften the surrounding text. View → Pages switches to a paginated view.

When the combination feels right, use Writing → Saved Writing Setups to keep your scene, sound, and text preferences together for another day.

06

Let your words go places.

When you’re ready to share, choose File → Export Writing. Export the chapter or paper you’re working on, or your whole book. Choose a format and save a copy where you want it.

  • PDF — a laid-out copy for reading, sharing, or printing.
  • Word (.docx) — a document you can continue working on in a word processor.
  • Markdown — plain text with your Markdown formatting.
  • EPUB — an ebook whose text adapts to the reader’s screen.

The preview shows the PDF layout. Word and ebook readers may arrange pages differently. Exporting creates a copy; your original writing stays in your Library.

07

Leave room for another draft.

Open Writing → Backups and Recovery to choose a backup folder and make a backup of your Library and recovery history. You can also enable daily backups while DumasWriter is open.

To revisit an earlier idea, open Writing → Draft History and Recovery. Restore an earlier version as a separate copy, then compare it with your current work.

Autosave keeps your latest changes. A backup gives you another copy. Choose a backup location that fits the way you already protect important files.

And whenever you need a small reminder, Help → Tutorial is there. For now, all you need is a first line.
